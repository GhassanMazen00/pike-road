# Pike Road: Story and Game Design

The full story with every blank filled in, plus how the game plays.
Owner's beats are kept; additions are marked **(new)**. Anything here can change. Tell me what to cut.

---

## The truth (what the player pieces together)

**October 1994, Hollow Pines County.** Amos Pike, 71, a widower, lives in the old Pike place with his
granddaughter **June**, 8. June's parents died two years earlier in a crash on Pike Road. Amos doesn't trust
banks, and the whole county knows he keeps money in the house.

On the night of **October 14, 1994**, two men break in: **Raymond Hale**, 24, and **Curtis Webb**, 27. Amos
fights back with a kitchen knife. **June hides in the upstairs bedroom closet.** They find her anyway.
Hale and Webb bury both of them in the woods behind the house and take the cash, Amos's **pocket watch**
and June's **music box**.

The young deputy who answers the call the next morning is **R. Haskins**, 23. He finds blood and no
one home. The bodies turn up that spring in shallow graves and are reburied in the Pike family plot in
the same woods. Webb dies in prison on another charge. **Hale is never charged**, moves away, and later
has a daughter: **Nora**.

**Why renters disappear.** Amos and June can't leave Pike Road. They're bound to their graves until
someone returns what was taken from them. Every renter gets "tested": the knocks, the friendly
neighbor, the girl with the pie. Whoever lets them in at night dies. A few renters worked out that the
dead "want their things back" and left notes, believing it would make the haunting stop.
**It doesn't. Bringing the things back sets the dead free.**

**Why Daniel (new).** When Daniel unpacks, he puts a framed wedding photo of Nora on the dresser. When
Amos visits ("just saying hi"), he sees it. The newspaper Daniel finds in Part 3 names the suspect:
**Raymond Hale**. Nora's maiden name is Hale. Amos and June stop trying to kill Daniel and start *using*
him. They need someone to return their things, and then someone to drive them to Hale's daughter.

The twist works without being spelled out. A sharp player catches the name "Hale" in the newspaper. Everyone
gets it at the end, when the last screen shows a clipping: *"Wedding: Daniel Reyes and Nora Hale."*

## Rules the player learns (setups that pay off)

1. **"Count the knocks after midnight. Don't answer them."** The dead always knock **three slow times**.
   Living people knock differently: Haskins pounds and calls out "Sheriff's office!" Attentive players can tell.
2. **"If a little girl comes to your door with a pie, don't you eat it."** Part 2 pays this off.
3. **"There aren't any neighbors on Pike Road. Not anymore."** So who is Amos?
4. **911 takes 40 minutes out here**, so the deputy's card in your wallet matters.
5. **The candles and matches** from the stand are needed at the graves in Part 3. *(new)*

## Part 1: The Drive, The House, The Night (about 35 min)

1. **Cold open.** A black screen, car interior sound, rain on glass. Texts from Nora read aloud by Daniel. Title card.
2. **The drive.** Night road, wipers, radio static between stations. In the headlights, *a little girl at the roadside*
   (June). She's gone when you look back.
3. **Gas station.** Buy a flashlight, batteries and a frozen dinner. Missing-person posters on the board.
   Marge reacts to "Pike Road." Deputy Haskins is buying coffee, separately. He hands over his card:
   "911's about forty minutes out here. Call me direct."
4. **Roadside stand.** The old woman gives candles and matches. "There aren't any neighbors on Pike Road. Not anymore."
   "If a little girl comes to your door with a pie, don't you eat it."
5. **Checkpoint.** The guard writes Daniel's name under the others. "Count the knocks after midnight. Don't answer them."
6. **Arrival and unpacking.** *Objectives:* carry the bags in, put food in the fridge, put clothes in the bedroom
   dresser, and **set Nora's photo on the dresser** *(new: this is the plant)*. Plug the phone into the kitchen charger.
7. **First wrong thing.** The front door swings open by itself. No one there. Close it.
8. **The knocks.** Microwave the dinner. Three knocks. Again every 5 to 10 seconds until he answers. An old man:
   "I'm your neighbor, just saying hi. Amos. My granddaughter June's around here somewhere."
   *His eyes go past Daniel to the photo on the dresser through the bedroom door. (new)* Daniel says thanks and closes the door.
9. **Nora's text.** She's sorry, she knows he needed time, she hopes they can fix things. The player picks one of two replies.
10. **Lights out.** Get the flashlight from the living room. Go out the back door to the fuse box. One fuse is missing.
11. **June.** On the way back she's standing in the dark yard. The dialogue is as written. She drops the fuse, laughs and runs into the trees.
    "This girl and her grandpa are so weird. I need to be careful."
12. **Fuse.** Put it back. Wandering triggers "I better go put back this fuse." Then go to bed.
13. **The TV.** He wakes to static. Amos and June are on the couch. The dialogue is as written, and the knife comes out. **Checkpoint.**
14. **Chase.** Upstairs, into the bedroom, **into the closet**. *June's whisper from inside the wall:
    "This was my hiding spot too." (new)* Getting caught means a full-screen death (see Scares).
15. **Hidden.** "I have to call someone." Wait for Amos to leave. Sneak downstairs for the phone. Call Haskins:
    "On my way. Stay hidden."
16. A door slams. Silence. "Maybe they left." Lock the front door. "I must get something to protect myself."
    **End of Part 1.**

## Part 2: The Basement (about 30 min)

As the owner wrote it, with these details:
- **Spatula:** in the kitchen utensil drawer. "Maybe something stronger in the basement."
- **The key:** the basement door is locked. The key is in the **china cabinet in the living room, inside the sugar bowl**.
  Lights can be switched on room by room while you search drawers and cabinets.
- **The knock (three slow knocks):** the Open / "Who's there?" / refuse branches as the owner wrote them. Opening
  without the spatula means June's death scare. With the spatula you hit her, and she shrieks and is gone.
  Repeated refusals mean the door bangs violently, then silence.
- **The basement:** dark concrete, a furnace, shelves, a workbench and a crawlspace. The flashlight is in one hand and the spatula in the other.
  **The gun (new): Amos's old hunting rifle with two shells.** It's in a locked gun cabinet. The cabinet key is in the
  workbench drawer under a rag. Finding both takes 4 to 6 minutes, and many players won't.
- **After 4 minutes:** knocking again, **fast and heavy**, with "Sheriff's office!" It's Haskins. Daniel names Amos and June.
  Haskins: "These two lived here. They're both dead, buried in the forest. You outsiders always fake a report
  and disappear." He leaves.
- **Nora's texts.** "It was a mistake coming here. I'm packing and coming back tonight." She answers: "I'll wait for you, not gonna sleep, babe."
  He starts packing. **End of Part 2.**

## Part 3: The Graves and Home (about 35 min)

1. **Packing.** In the bedroom dresser he finds the **renters' notes** *(new: three handwritten notes, one per missing renter,
   each closer to the "truth")*. In the hall closet there's a **1994 newspaper**: "PIKE GRANDFATHER, GIRL MISSING. Police seek
   Raymond Hale, 24, for questioning."
   The last note: *"They want their things back. The watch and the music box. Put them on the graves and light a candle for
   each. Then they'll let you go."*
2. **The search (new).** Two items, with Amos and June hunting you through the house:
   - **Amos's pocket watch:** in the basement crawlspace, in a tin (where Hale hid what he couldn't sell).
   - **June's music box:** in June's old room upstairs, behind a loose wall panel. Opening it plays her tune.
     That's dangerous, because she comes.
   - Your **car keys are missing**. They were on the kitchen counter.
3. **The forest.** Out the back door, follow the old path to the family plot. Fog, the flashlight, hiding behind and in
   hollow trees. **The rifle** (if found): one shot makes a ghost vanish for a few seconds. It doesn't kill. Two shells, two escapes.
4. **The graves.** Place the watch on Amos's stone and the music box on June's. Light a candle on each with the matches.
   **These are the stand's gifts.** The candles flare. June appears, throws him **his car keys**, smiles, and both are gone.
   *(An optional fourth note is nailed to a tree near the graves, Tom Adair's last page: "I think it's what they wanted. I
   think I let them OUT." Found too late.)*
5. **The drive home.** A short drive at dawn, then the city.
6. **The apartment.** "I'm here." Her reply: "Good, I'm waiting for you upstairs under the bed." "Under the bed? What are you saying?" No answer.
   The apartment is messy and bloody, and you can explore it. The bedroom. Nora under the bed. "No… Nora."
7. He stands. Amos and June are there. "Thanks for releasing us into this world. It's time for us to punish those who hurt us."
   **Death. Black.** A newspaper clipping fades in: *"Wedding: Daniel Reyes and Nora Hale."* Credits.

## How it plays

- **First person, story-driven, about 1.5 to 2 hours in total.** Each part is playable on its own from the main menu once unlocked.
- **Objectives** in the top-left, short and plain ("Put the groceries away").
- **Everyday tasks first.** Carrying bags and microwaving dinner make the scares land harder.
- **Phone (Tab):** texts from Nora and calls. Some replies are choices, which only change wording.
- **Reading:** notes and the newspaper open full-screen with a readable typed version.
- **Hands:** flashlight (F) in the left hand. Spatula or rifle in the right.
- **Hiding:** closets, under the bed, behind and inside hollow trees. **You're safe unless the enemy saw you go in.** No breath-holding.
- **Chases:** Amos is slower than your sprint but never stops. June doesn't chase. She appears.
- **Deaths:** quick, loud and very scary (a full-face grab, a sharp sting, a cut to black). You restart at the last
  checkpoint with no menus in between.
- **Checkpoints and auto-save** at every story beat. Chapter select after finishing a part.
- **Settings:** volume (master, music, effects, voices), mouse sensitivity, FOV, subtitles size, screen effects on or off.

## Scares (the rules)
- Silence before every scare. No music telling you something is coming.
- Few jump scares. Each part has one or two big ones, and the rest is dread.
- June is never seen walking. She's just *there* when you turn around.
- No blinding screen effects. Chase effects are a faint vignette and heartbeat audio only.

## Audio direction
- **Every sound is a real recording**: footsteps per surface (wood, carpet, tile, concrete, gravel, leaves, porch boards), with a set of
  6 to 10 per surface for walking, running and stairs.
- **Each room sounds like a room**: reverb zones (a small bedroom, the tiled bathroom, the concrete basement, the open forest).
- **Room tone** is always present, even in "silence". The house creaks, the fridge hums, the clock ticks.
- Sounds through walls are muffled.
- Music only at title cards and the credits.
