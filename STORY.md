# Pike Road: Story and Game Design (v2)

Version 2 follows the owner's new direction: a witch's house, a dead daughter, guilt, and slow-building
psychological horror. The grandfather, June and the break-in are gone.
Lines marked **(new)** are my additions to fill the blanks. Change anything.

---

## The truth (what the player pieces together)

**A year ago** Daniel Reyes was driving his daughter **Lily** (7) home at night. Nora was texting him,
angry about something small, and he looked down to answer. A deer stood in the road. He swerved, the car
rolled, and Lily died. The clock on the dash stopped at **3:07**.

Since then Daniel and Nora have only fought. Each blames the other: *you were texting me*, *you were
looking at your phone*. They're both right.

**Agnes Pike** was the old woman who lived on Pike Road a long time ago. People said she was a witch.
She died in 1978, and the house she lived in still does what she did: it **draws in grieving people and
feeds on them**. Her bargain has always been the same. She brings back the dead person you miss, a
little at a time, until you will do anything to have them back. Then she asks for one thing, and the
dead come back for real, but **as she made them: angry**.

The rental listing found Daniel, not the other way around: *"A quiet place to grieve. Pike Road. $40 a night."*

**Lily is the ghost.** Daniel doesn't let himself believe it until Night 2. She heard every fight her
parents had about her death. What comes back is hurt, then angry, then something else.

**Agnes is the "neighbor"**: a sweet old woman who visits, gives advice and slowly guides Daniel to the
ritual. The ritual doesn't set Lily free. It brings her **home**.

**Ending:** Daniel drives home at dawn with something humming in the back seat. Nora texts: *"I'm waiting
for you upstairs under the bed."* Nora is dead. Lily: **"Now we're all together."** She kills him.
Final card: the rental listing again, with new dates. *(The house waits for the next one.)*

## How the fear works (design rules)

1. **Deniable, then personal, then undeniable, then hostile.** Every event should be explainable at first
   (wind, an old house, tiredness). Then the events become about *him* (Lily's things, the 3:07 time).
   Only then do they become impossible. Only on the last nights do they become dangerous.
2. **Grief is the monster.** The scariest objects are ordinary: a child's car seat, a seatbelt chime,
   a drawing, a lullaby, a phone. The player's own memories are used against him.
3. **Silence before every event.** The ambience fades out for 2 to 3 seconds. Players learn to fear quiet.
4. **Things happen when you look away.** Events trigger when the camera is turned away and are discovered
   when you turn back. The rear-view mirror and real mirrors show what you can't see directly.
5. **Sound behind the player.** Whispers, humming and footsteps are placed in 3D right behind the
   listener and move when you turn.
6. **Repetition with one detail changed.** Walk down the same hallway twice. The second time one thing is wrong.
7. **3:07.** Clocks stop at it, the phone shows it and the radio station is 103.7. The player learns the number.
8. **Few jump scares, earned.** One or two per night, always after a long build.
9. **No blinding screen effects.** The camera does the work: a slow tilt, a heartbeat, ringing ears after a
   shock, short slow motion, breath, a vignette at most.

## Structure: three nights

### Night 1, "The Drive" (about 35 min)

**1. The road (cold open).** Black screen. The hum of an old engine, tires on asphalt, a turn signal ticking.
Fade in on Daniel's **hands on the wheel** of his late father's old sedan. His own car was wrecked in the
crash. A dark two-lane road with forest on both sides. The dash glows a dim green. **(new)** In the rear-view
mirror there's a **child car seat** in the back. It's never explained. He couldn't take it out.
- The player steers gently along a single route, with the car held to the road, and can look around.
  The radio knob tunes stations: a late-night talk show about loss, old country music, static.

**2. Hollow Pines Gas & Grocery.** A buzzing sign, moths and a payphone ringing outside that nobody
answers. A man sits on the bench by the ice machine, not moving.
- **Walt (the clerk):** "Evening. Don't get many new faces this late. Where you headed?" Daniel: "Pike Road.
  A rental." Walt stops what he's doing. "...Pike Road." Pause. "Power goes out a lot up there. Old lines.
  You'll want a flashlight. Batteries too." Later, quieter: "Folks who rent that place... they come through
  here sad." *(He doesn't finish.)*
  **(new)** He adds two candles to the bag. "On the house. Old place like that." *(Needed for the ritual.)*
- **Deputy Haskins** walks in for coffee. "Don't recognize you." Daniel tells him where he's going. Haskins
  and Walt exchange a look. "Pike Road. Well. Dispatch is about forty minutes out from there. Here."
  He writes his cell number on a receipt. "Anything at all. You call me, not 911."
- **Buy:** a frozen dinner, the flashlight and batteries.
- **Walking out:** the bench is empty. The man is standing at **Daniel's car, looking in the back window at
  the car seat.** "She's cold back there." He walks off into the dark past the pumps. The payphone stops ringing.

**3. The drive on.** Fog thickens. A text from Nora: *"Text me when you get there. I mean it."* The player
picks a reply.
- **The deer.** On a bend, the radio cuts to silence: a full second with no engine noise. The headlights
  catch a **deer standing in the road**. *Impact.* Half a second of slow motion, the camera jolts, the
  windshield cracks, then ringing ears, a heartbeat and breathing. This is the same thing that killed Lily.
- **Choice: Get out and check / Keep driving.**
  - **Get out:** walk back in the red taillight glow. **There's nothing**: no deer, no blood, no fur. The
    crack in the windshield is real. Faint humming comes from the trees behind you, the lullaby. Back in
    the car, the **rear seatbelt is buckled across the empty car seat**, and the chime is dinging.
  - **Keep driving:** the seatbelt chime starts on its own a minute later.

**4. Pike Road.** Turn onto a dirt road through dense forest. Gravel under the tires and branches scraping
the doors. A leaning mailbox says PIKE. The **house** appears through the trees: old, sagging, dark. The porch
light **flickers on as you pull up** (a motion sensor?). The engine goes off. Silence. Title card: **PIKE ROAD**.

**5. Moving in.** Normal tasks: carry the bags, put the dinner in the freezer, set the flashlight down. **(new)**
**Put Lily's photo somewhere.** Daniel brought a framed photo but can't look at it. The player chooses where
to put it, face down or face up. *(The house remembers. Later it's turned toward his bed.)*
- On the fridge there's an old **child's drawing**: a car, three people and a sun with a face. Daniel says
  "previous tenants." Players who know will recognize Lily's sun later.
- Text Nora: "I'm here." Her reply is cold. The fight starts again over text, and the backstory comes out
  in pieces.

**6. The neighbor.** Three soft knocks. An old woman with a covered dish: **Agnes**. "Welcome, dear. I live
just through the trees, saw your lights." She's warm, a little too warm.
- "You've got a sadness on you. I can always tell."
- **(new)** "Don't go into the woods after dark, dear. The path moves."
- "Sometimes the ones we lose aren't as far as we think. This house... it listens."
- She leaves. Walt said there aren't any neighbors out here. *(The player may remember.)*

**7. 3:07 AM.** Daniel wakes in the dark. The clock reads 3:07. Outside, **the car's seatbelt chime**
is ringing and the car's dome light is on. He goes out in the dark with the flashlight. The back door of
the car is open and the car seat is buckled. He closes it and goes back in.
- In the hallway, very soft, from somewhere dark: **"Daddy?"** Then nothing. **End of Night 1.**

### Night 2, "The House" (about 45 min): investigation and escalation

- **Events escalate**: footsteps upstairs while he's downstairs. Doors that were closed are open. The
  photo of Lily is now **face up, turned toward his bed**. New drawings appear on the fridge, of the
  crash, a deer, and a tall woman with long fingers.
- **The little room upstairs** is decorated almost exactly like Lily's bedroom at home. Same night light,
  same wallpaper pattern. **(new)** It wasn't like that last night.
- **Forest trip 1:** follow children's laughter into the trees. Stick charms and **witch bottles** hang
  from branches, and they mark the path. Find a **small pink shoe**: Lily's, lost in the crash. **Daniel
  realizes.** "...Lily?"
- **Calling Haskins:** he comes and finds nothing. On the porch he says Agnes Pike died in 1978, and that
  everyone who rents this place has "lost someone." He leaves uneasy.
- **Puzzles:**
  - **The radio:** the old radio in the study, tuned to **103.7**, plays a recording of Daniel's own voice
    singing the lullaby. The tune is the clue to the music box.
  - **Lily's drawings:** count the crows in each drawing to get the basement **combination lock**.
  - **The basement:** Agnes's things are down there: jars, herbs, her journal. **Journal pages** explain the
    bargain in her words.
- **Forest trip 2:** the path moves. Ribbons show the true way, and a wrong turn loops you back to the
  house, which scares you more each time. You reach **Agnes's grave** and a stone circle.
- **Agnes's second visit**, at the tree line, not the door: "There's a way to set her free, dear. Bring
  me three things." *(The ritual items.)*
- **End of Night 2:** the power goes out. The fuse box is outside. Coming back, **Lily is at the end of
  the upstairs hall**, wrong now. She comes. **Hide in the closet.** (Hiding: you're safe unless she saw
  you go in.) The first real chase.

### Night 3, "The Ritual" (about 40 min)

- **The three things:** *"Something she loved"* is her stuffed rabbit, which Daniel brought and keeps in the
  car. *"Something of the night it happened"* is the stopped dash clock from the wrecked car, in his
  suitcase. *"Something of you"* is his blood: a cut hand in the kitchen.
- **The hidden room:** the stuck bathroom door was never a bathroom. Set the grandfather clock to **3:07**
  and it opens: Agnes's room, with the ritual knife and the candles' place.
- **The worst of the haunting:** Lily and Agnes both hunt now. There are short chases, hiding and the
  mirror scares. The house is darker and older each time you return.
- **Forest trip 3, the ritual:** the stone circle at 3:07. Place the three things and light Walt's two
  candles. Lily appears, small and calm: **"Can I come home now, Daddy?"** The player must choose
  **"Yes."** (The only choice. *That's the horror.*)
- **The drive home at dawn:** the windshield is still cracked. The seatbelt chime. Humming from the back seat.
  You don't look, and the mirror is turned away. **(new)** If you turn it back, the car seat is empty.
- **The apartment:** "I'm here." *"Good, I'm waiting for you upstairs under the bed."* "Under the bed?
  What are you saying?" No answer. The apartment is wrecked and bloody, and you can explore it.
  The bedroom. Nora is under the bed. "No... Nora."
- Lily, behind him: **"Now we're all together."** Black.
- **Final card:** the rental listing, with new dates. Credits.

## How it plays

- **First person, about 2 hours in total.** Three nights, each playable from the menu once unlocked.
- **Objectives** in the top-left corner, short and plain.
- **Phone (Tab):** Nora's texts, which tell the backstory, and calls to Haskins.
- **Items:** flashlight, batteries, candles, the photo, the shoe, keys, the music box and the ritual items.
- **Puzzles:** the fuse box, the radio frequency, the combination from the drawings, the clock set to 3:07,
  and finding the true forest path.
- **Hiding:** closets, under the bed and behind trees. Safe unless she saw you go in. No breath-holding.
- **Deaths:** quick and shocking, then restart at the last checkpoint.
- **Settings:** volume per channel, mouse sensitivity, field of view, subtitle size, screen effects on or off.
